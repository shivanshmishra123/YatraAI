# Complete Travel Guide to Jaipur (The Pink City)

## Overview & History
Jaipur, the capital of Rajasthan, was founded in 1727 by Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II. Named 'The Pink City' after Maharaja Ram Singh painted the entire city terracotta pink in 1876 to welcome Prince Albert, Jaipur forms part of India's famous Golden Triangle circuit alongside Delhi and Agra.

## Best Time to Visit
- **October to March**: The winter months are pleasant with daytime temperatures between 18°C and 25°C. Ideal for heritage walks and palace visits.
- **April to June**: Summer can be severe with temperatures exceeding 42°C. Not recommended unless attending indoor cultural festivals.
- **July to September**: Monsoon brings relief and turns the Aravalli hills surrounding Amer Fort lush green.

## Top Attractions & Heritage Sites
1. **Amber (Amer) Fort & Palace**: Located 11 km from the city center, this hilltop fortress showcases Mughal-Rajput architecture, the famous Sheesh Mahal (Mirror Palace), and Maota Lake views. Recommended entry time: 8:30 AM before tourist crowds.
2. **Hawa Mahal (Palace of Winds)**: A five-story pink sandstone facade with 953 jharokhas (small lattice windows) designed for royal ladies to view street processions without being observed. Best photographed in morning light from across the street.
3. **City Palace**: The royal residence featuring Chandra Mahal, Mubarak Mahal, and museum galleries displaying historic armor, textiles, and royal carriages.
4. **Jantar Mantar**: UNESCO World Heritage site and the world's largest stone astronomical observatory featuring the Vrihat Samrat Yantra sundial with 2-second accuracy.
5. **Nahargarh Fort & Jaigarh Fort**: Sunset panoramic views over Jaipur city from Padao restaurant at Nahargarh Fort. Jaigarh houses Jaivana, once the world's largest cannon on wheels.
6. **Albert Hall Museum**: Indo-Saracenic museum illuminated stunningly at night, located in Ram Niwas Garden.
7. **Galta Ji (Monkey Temple)**: Historic pilgrimage site set in a mountain pass with natural sacred water tanks (kunds).

## Transportation & Connectivity
- **Railways**: Jaipur Junction (JP) and Gandhinagar Jaipur (GADJ) connect to Delhi via high-speed Vande Bharat Express (approx 3.5 hours), Ajmer Shatabdi, and Double Decker Express.
- **Highway (NH-48 & Delhi-Mumbai Expressway)**: Delhi to Jaipur takes around 3.5 to 4 hours via the newly commissioned Delhi-Mumbai Expressway (NE-4) junction.
- **Local Transit**: Jaipur Metro covers Mansarovar to Badi Chaupar (directly connecting Chandpole and Old City). E-rickshaws and auto-rickshaws are plentiful; agree on fare or use meter/ride-hailing apps.

## Culinary Specialties & Iconic Eateries
- **Dal Baati Churma**: Authentic Rajasthani meal with ghee-soaked baked wheat balls, spiced lentils, and sweet churma. Top spots: Laxmi Misthan Bhandar (LMB) in Johari Bazaar and Rawat Mishthan Bhandar near the railway station.
- **Pyaaz Kachori & Mawa Kachori**: Rawat Mishthan Bhandar serves legendary crispy onion kachoris.
- **Ghewar & Malpua**: Traditional disc-shaped honeycomb sweet especially popular during Teej and Gangaur festivals. Available at LMB and Sambhar Fini.
- **Laal Maas**: Fiery mutton curry prepared with Mathania red chilies, signature of royal Rajput dining. Handi Restaurant and Niros on MI Road are renowned.
- **Lassi**: Served in clay kulhads at Lassiwala (Shop 312, MI Road) operating since 1944.

## Shopping & Bazaars
- **Johari Bazaar**: World-renowned for Kundan jewelry, Meenakari enamel work, and precious gemstones.
- **Bapu Bazaar & Nehru Bazaar**: Traditional Mojari leather shoes, camel leather craft, block-printed cotton bedsheets, and bandhani tie-dye sarees.
- **Tripolia Bazaar**: Famous for traditional lac bangles crafted by artisans in Maniharon Ka Rasta.

## Practical Travel Tips & Etiquette
- Composite entry tickets are available at Amber Fort covering Hawa Mahal, Jantar Mantar, Nahargarh, and Albert Hall at discounted rates for domestic travelers and students.
- Always dress respectfully when visiting temples and stepwells (cover shoulders and knees).
- Bargaining is customary in traditional bazaars; politely negotiate 20% to 30% below initial vendor quote.
