# Sacred Sites & Temple Etiquette Guide for Travelers in India

## Core Etiquette Across Major Traditions
1. **Footwear Removal**: Always remove shoes, sandals, and socks before stepping onto temple premises. Designated safe shoe-keeping counters (Joota Ghar) are available at temple gates (often free or ₹5–₹10 fee).
2. **Attire & Modesty**:
   - Cover shoulders and knees. In traditional South Indian temples (e.g., Padmanabhaswamy Temple in Thiruvananthapuram, Guruvayur), men must wear a traditional white dhoti (mundu) and enter bare-chested; women wear sarees or long traditional skirts.
   - At Sikh Gurdwaras (such as Golden Temple in Amritsar), both men and women must cover their heads with a scarf or rumal and wash their feet in the shallow water channel before entering.
3. **Circumambulation (Pradakshina)**: When walking around inner shrines or sanctums, always walk in a **clockwise** direction so your right side faces the deity.
4. **Prasad (Sacred Offering)**: Accept prasad offered by priests with your **right hand** (or cupped hands with right hand over left). Never use only the left hand.
5. **Photography Rules**: Sanctum sanctorum (Garbhagriha) photography is strictly prohibited in virtually all Hindu, Jain, and Sikh holy sites. Respect privacy during prayers.
